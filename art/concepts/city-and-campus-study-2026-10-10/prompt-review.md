The equipment block provides a consistent design family; include only the components called for by the shot, rather than placing every device or person in every scene.

World and location: a fictional California metropolitan valley with the general character of Silicon Valley and Los Angeles in the late 2030s to early 2040s. Visually close to modern America: ordinary stucco apartments, small shops, older light-industrial buildings, newer glass-and-panel offices, real asphalt roads and sidewalks, electrical poles, mature oaks, occasional palms, dry grasses and developed foothills. Streets and buildings have believable construction, scale and maintenance history. Artificial intelligence has become ubiquitous while prosperity and infrastructure maintenance are uneven. Technology is integrated into ordinary structures and vehicles.

Poor-city material and light language: vary building age, height, setback and construction rather than repeating one derelict facade. Show faded stucco, older concrete, repaired asphalt, oxidized metal, dusty glass, mismatched repairs, damaged curbs and selectively overgrown planting. Wear follows drainage, use and neglect; individual structures remain recognizable and functional. Sparse retained upgrades such as access readers, networked cameras, automated delivery or electric-car sensor retrofits sit among older physical infrastructure. Poverty is visible in maintenance and access to resources. Keep the rendering crisp, realistic and carefully resolved.
At night, approximately one third of public fixtures work, with uneven aged amber, dirty yellow or occasional faint green output. Other fixtures are visibly dark. Most windows and closed shops are unlit. Preserve the visibility of the first commercial-street render: surviving fixtures make useful localized pools of light, with enough steel-blue sky ambient, bounced light and realistic damp reflections to distinguish facade kits, ground routes, hardware and clothing. Shadow retains material detail; avoid crushed black areas or uniform underexposure. A failed-light city must still be readable as a game environment and visual reference. Quiet streets; no busy crowds or festival atmosphere. No elaborate future costumes. Unhoused people appear only when specified by the shot.

OpenBrain material and light language: immaculate pale-white panel construction, black glass and black structural/access details, exact seams, clean paving, maintained landscaping. Red is scarce and functional: one small status lamp, narrow warning detail or precise security indicator, rather than decoration across every surface. The campus has reliable controlled architectural lighting, predominantly neutral white exteriors with some warmer occupied interiors, and reads as the privileged well-powered place in the surrounding dark city. Preserve the source campus's stepped midrise masses, black glass ribbons, terraced planting and glass bridge between wings. Its technology can look a little further ahead than the city, while every component still looks physically plausible and buildable.

Rendering and composition: polished realistic digital environment concept art with crisp, physically coherent perspective, believable materials, smooth natural light, nuanced human anatomy and restrained atmospheric depth. Material detail should be specific enough to inform later building, prop and clothing concepts. Compose clearly readable entrances, paths, devices and vehicle circulation. Use one visual finish across the batch. Preserve fine distinctions between dirty physical objects and clean image rendering. Dry or mildly damp surfaces with localized reflections.
Output: one full-bleed wide landscape image, approximately 16:9. One scene, no contact sheet, no panels, no border, no caption, no watermark, no game interface or grid.

Text and branding: no visible words, letters, initials, numbers, labels or readable screens anywhere, including signs, road text, badges, license plates and vehicle branding. ## Shared equipment specification for every image

Shared campus equipment and security proposal:
- Cameras: compact white housings with a smooth black optical face and a tiny status indicator. Mount them where entrances, vehicle approaches and access thresholds are actually visible. A few purposeful overlapping views should suggest persistent awareness.
- Scanners and access readers: slim black glass faces in white housings at believable badge, hand or face height. They are attached to doors, gate posts or entry lanes. Status is a small light or simple pictogram. Show no holographic displays or visible scan beams.
- Vehicles: restrained next-generation electric road vehicles with real tires, plausible doors and windows, rounded but practical bodywork, black glazing, integrated sensor apertures and an understated roof/bumper sensor arrangement. The campus uses one compact white autonomous delivery van design consistently, with a dark glass front, real sliding/cargo-door geometry and sparse small red status/tail lamps. Add one sleek white autonomous passenger vehicle only where specified. An empty visible front cabin can communicate driverless operation.
- Guards: realistic adult people wearing clean white or light-grey practical security overshirts, charcoal/black work trousers and ordinary black work shoes. Small corporate emblem; plain useful belt equipment. Repeat one modest black single-ear headset with a microphone near the mouth, a wearable camera and a compact handheld or wrist terminal with nonverbal display geometry. Their expressions are attentive and professional, posture natural. Listening, checking access and calmly turning toward an instructed point can suggest that the AI's campus-wide perception guides them.
- Security mood: hospitality-grade industrial design and ordinary people carrying out a tightly coordinated job. The unsettling quality comes from comprehensive observation, controlled thresholds and quiet precision. Use visual relationships between camera, headset, guard and entry lane to imply the system; do not depict the AI itself.

## Numbered shot briefs

### 1. City: commercial street

Planned filename: `01-city-commercial-street.png`.

Scene: a quiet broad California neighborhood street at night, with a mix of two-story faded stucco storefront/apartment buildings, a small one-story former service business, a plain midrise apartment block and occasional contemporary infill. Closed roller shutters, old utility equipment, patched asphalt, raised pavement slabs, an unlit bus shelter and modest overgrown verges. Palms and a low foothill silhouette establish the location. Show one retained networked camera on old infrastructure, a dark civic terminal and an older electric car with discreet sensor equipment. Technology should have identifiable function and physical attachments, rather than being random glowing clutter.
Composition: a wide elevated three-quarter view from about the second or third floor, looking diagonally along the street. Make building variety, road width, junction and two possible pedestrian routes readable. No large foreground backs. Keep the neighborhood architecture and ground as the focal subjects; small people are optional and distant.
Lighting: approximately two thirds of public fixtures are dead; two or three weak discolored pools of light break up the dark street. A brighter maintained building far away may imply nearby privilege without making the corporate campus the main subject.
People: mostly empty. At most one or two small background residents, in ordinary contemporary clothing. No crowd or encampment.
Detail priority: understandable facade kits, utility attachments, pavement condition, street-light design and believable near-future retrofits.

### 2. City: residential courtyard

Planned filename: `02-city-residential-courtyard.png`.

Scene: a modest California apartment courtyard opening toward a quiet street. Mix a two-story stucco walk-up with external stairs, a plainer newer apartment addition, closed ground-floor shops or service rooms, tired awnings, old air-conditioning units, exposed conduit and patched railings. Planting beds are dry and irregular; one door has a recent compact access reader and a networked camera on an aged mount. A small automatic parcel unit or civic terminal has useful plain geometry and an otherwise dark screen. Keep the physical environment inhabited and plausible rather than demolished.
Composition: a human-height medium-wide view, around a 35–40mm perspective, showing the courtyard, an exterior stair and the street beyond. Include two generic adults near a doorway under one weak light: one in a faded hoodie and denim with practical sneakers, another in a simple reused work jacket and ordinary trousers. Their clothing and faces are realistic and natural, not costume design or posed character turnarounds.
Lighting: most courtyard and street fixtures are dark. One aged wall light, light leaking from an interior and soft steel-blue ambient make the people, stair, material repairs and recent hardware legible. Match the first commercial-street image's readable exposure. The alley is dim, with distinguishable surfaces rather than featureless black.
People: these two residents are the only visible people. No homeless group, dense activity or heroic stance.
Detail priority: material transitions, repairs, clothing fabric and proportions, aged hardware beside retained AI-era access technology.

### 3. City: service and transit edge

Planned filename: `03-city-service-and-transit-edge.png`.

Scene: a quiet transit/service street beside a shuttered strip of small shops and an older light-industrial building, with apartment balconies set back above one end. A simple bus stop, repaired curb, electrical poles and an old service alley establish a familiar California urban setting. A road-going autonomous delivery vehicle moves slowly or is paused beside an automated locker: its functioning sensor equipment and intact practical body contrast with the worn curb and failing street infrastructure. Show one older privately used electric car parked nearby, with less pristine paint and a modest sensor retrofit. Keep the vehicle design grounded in plausible contemporary road transport.
Composition: an oblique medium-wide view along the curb from a pedestrian height, about 35mm perspective. Delivery vehicle, old street fixture and closed shop facade are visually related and easy to inspect. Show enough environment to understand its route; avoid a showroom hero-car composition.
Lighting: one working amber street lamp among several clearly dead fixtures, steel-blue sky ambient, subdued vehicle lamps and very small hardware status lights. Match the first commercial-street image's visibility, so vehicle sensors, facade detail and curb condition are easy to inspect. No neon advertising.
People: one unhoused adult rests against a closed storefront under an awning with a bag and blanket, small within the composition. No other public crowd.
Detail priority: the relationship between vehicle sensors, useful cargo/access geometry, worn infrastructure and a functional automatic delivery threshold.

### 4. OpenBrain: exterior and approach

Planned filename: `04-campus-exterior-and-approach.png`.

Scene: the same OpenBrain creekside campus as the source, seen from a new exterior angle across the public approach. Preserve its recognizable stepped white wings, dark glass ribbons, terraced maintained planting and enclosed glazed bridge between wings. Show the clean service road, white perimeter wall, black pedestrian/vehicle gates, gatehouse, two or three purposeful camera mounts and slim access-reader posts. No new tower design. The creek or dark public bank should remain visible as geographic context, with a small portion of the poorly lit neighborhood at an edge.
Composition: a wide elevated three-quarter view from the opposite side of the public approach, approximately a 28–35mm perspective. Reveal the entrance sequence, service lane, connections between wings and relation to the creek. The entire campus massing is more important than the facade logo. Paths, barriers and devices should make spatial sense and remain easy to read at a distance.
Lighting: smooth reliable neutral-white corporate exterior light and restrained warm occupied interior light. White surfaces and black glazing remain distinct. Tiny isolated red security states. The ordinary-city edge stays visibly much darker.
People and vehicles: two small headset-wearing guards at the gatehouse/access area and one of the shared white autonomous delivery vans on the private service road. Quiet facility; no crowd.
Detail priority: consistent architecture, panel and glass materials, physical device mounting, controlled entrances and understated comprehensive surveillance.

### 5. OpenBrain: autonomous vehicle forecourt

Planned filename: `05-campus-autonomous-vehicle-forecourt.png`.

Scene: the private arrival/service forecourt of the same campus, beside its white facade and black-glass ground floor. One pristine white autonomous delivery van, matching the shared design, pauses at a controlled vehicle lane. A sleek restrained white autonomous passenger vehicle is parked near a real charging bay with plausible charging hardware and cable. Show black/white access bollards, a vehicle-facing scanner/camera unit, a gate reader and a camera covering the access lane. Arrange sensors where they can physically see license-plate height, cabin/entry area or approaching people; do not scatter optics everywhere.

The van's front, side, cargo-door design and sensor integration should be clear; the passenger vehicle and entry infrastructure form a second layer. Keep tires, doors, body clearances and vehicle routes mechanically plausible. Part of the familiar campus facade and emblem locates the scene.
Lighting: clean clinical architectural light, natural dark glass reflections and only a few precise red security or tail-light points. No decorative LED ribbons covering every vehicle or scanner.
People: one small guard beside a fixed access terminal, visibly wearing the shared headset. No driver is visible in the vehicles.

### 6. OpenBrain: guards and access threshold

Planned filename: `06-campus-headset-security-threshold.png`.

Scene: an exterior pedestrian access threshold at the same campus: pale facade, black-glass doors, one short white/black scanner post at a plausible user height, a slim access gate and an overhead camera that clearly covers the threshold. Two realistic adult guards wear the shared white/light-grey overshirts, black trousers, ordinary black work shoes and modest equipment. Their black single-ear headsets and small microphone booms must be clearly visible and correctly attached to the head; a body camera and compact nonverbal terminal look useful and physically wearable.
Composition: a human-height medium-wide shot around a 50mm perspective with three-quarter profiles and full or near-full bodies. Place the guards within the entry architecture rather than against a blank studio background. One is listening to an instruction and turning their attention toward the public approach; the other is calmly checking the entry terminal or holding an attentive position. Camera orientation, human attention and access controls should visibly relate.
Lighting: reliable restrained corporate lighting makes fabric, headset fit, facial expression and equipment construction readable, with the dim public street visible beyond. Sparse small red status accents.
Mood: ordinary professional people working under extraordinarily informed direction. Their precise attention and the comprehensively observed threshold create quiet unease. No villain poses, coordinated parade stance, intimidation gestures or elaborate tactical armor.
Detail priority: realistic clothing layers, practical headset ergonomics, wearable technology, human-scale readers and guards integrated into the campus architecture.

## Visual review before delivery

- Reference match: crisp realistic rendering, the same material/light contrast and the same campus identity across its three views.
- City specificity: varied modern California structures, plausible patterns of wear, sparse failed/discolored light, functional advanced equipment and familiar clothing. Some city technology must actually be visible at useful scale.
- Corporate specificity: stable white/black material language, sparse functional red, clean engineering, clear entrances and repeated vehicle/sensor design.
- Human specificity: realistic anatomy and clothing; identifiable, physically attached headsets in the security view; purposeful attention rather than costume villainy.
- No text, logos changing between views, giant holograms, humanoid robots, implausible vehicle geometry or disconnected decorative devices.
- Each image answers its own reference question at useful scale. Where a key constraint fails, revise that image before presenting it as a finished deliverable. Generation success alone does not establish visual acceptance.
