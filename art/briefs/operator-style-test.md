# Briefs: the Operators' style test

Bryson's call (2026-10-13): the people are stylized, neither anime nor realistic; the world stays
realistic. Two styles to compare, painterly and clean hero. This test locks the style before any
outfit is redesigned, so the outfit stays the same in every image and only the style changes.
Exploratory: images go in `art/concepts/`, and nobody models from them.

The first concepts ([Request 19](../concepts/operator-looks-2026-10-12/README.md)) are what to move
away from: realistic, drab, and dressed to act out a backstory. The goal now is that he looks cool.

## The two styles

- **Painterly**, after Dishonored and Deathloop (Arkane): grounded but sculpted. Slightly
  exaggerated proportions (bigger hands, a strong jaw, a defined silhouette), hand-painted textures
  with visible brushwork, a rich but controlled palette.
- **Clean hero**, after Overwatch and Valorant: bold readable shapes, simplified materials, crisp
  edges, clear colour blocking. Made to read in an instant.
- **The blend**: painterly textures on clean hero shapes.

## The subject: the protagonist

The tradesman: he, early 30s, Mexican-American, warm tan skin, short dark curls, a sturdy build
(Astra's proposal from Request 19, kept so the comparison is about style). One simple cool outfit,
not final:

- A fitted dark charcoal canvas work jacket with a safety-orange collar lining and orange stitching,
  sleeves pushed up. A black tee, dark work pants, sturdy boots.
- A sleek half-face respirator with orange filter caps and amber-tinted glasses: his mask.
- His AI backpack: compact, clean-built, neat cable runs and protected heat vents. No screen, face or
  glow; the AI has no appearance.
- Orange is his one strong colour. Nothing white with red: that's OpenBrain.
