# Brief: the Operators, revised — personality, the writer's face, and their computers

Request 22. Bryson's notes on Request 21 (2026-10-13), turned into a brief for Astra. Exploratory:
the images go in `art/concepts/`, and nobody models from them yet.

## What Bryson said

- The three wear basically the same outfit. Give each one more personality.
- The writer should look more feminine: longer hair, a face like the reference he attaches. The
  reference is for the face only, not the outfit.
- Their backpacks should reflect their backstories: DIY, cheap and old, newer and sleeker.
- The packs aren't cloth backpacks. They're computers worn on the back, built to look like a cool
  backpack, with a 360° camera on a stalk so the AI can see too. Make that look cool in each
  version.
- Help Astra understand the story, the time period and what we're going for (below).

## The world they live in

The full story is in [STORY.md](../../STORY.md). What matters for how they look:

- **Around 2046, in a California city.** Visually close to today: no sci-fi fabrics, no neon,
  no armour. AI took the knowledge jobs; the basic income doesn't keep people housed. Most of the
  city is the poor part of town. Clothes are worn, repaired, secondhand. New things are expensive,
  and computers are worth more than gold.
- **They're in the resistance.** Anonymous: code names, covered faces, small groups reshuffled
  often. Cameras are everywhere, so covering the face is survival, not style. These three are
  strangers on their first mission together.
- **Their AI partners have no body and no face**, only text and voice. The pack is the AI's home:
  no screen, no face, no glowing eye. The camera is a sensor, not a head.
- **The enemy is OpenBrain**: pristine white with black and red. Nothing on the team is white with
  red.
- **The look:** stylized characters in a realistic world, exactly the rendering of
  [the protagonist's sheet](../reference/operator-protagonist-style.png). Cool first. The
  backstory shows in a few chosen details, not a costume.

## The three of them

### The tradesman — the protagonist (he, early 30s)

Works at a data center, as an electrician; his father owns a small electrical subcontractor. The
most comfortable of the three: his family never had to sell its data. Knows how a data center's
cooling works.

- **Outfit: keep it.** The adopted sheet stays as it is: zip work jacket, cargo pants, boots,
  respirator, orange.
- **Change only his pack and its camera** (below).

### The writer (she, early 30s)

Black, deep brown skin. Her journalism degree became useless the moment she finished it, when AI
took over the news. Broke. Now she writes and prints the resistance's illegal newspapers.

- **Face and hair: more feminine.** The face follows the reference Bryson attaches. Longer hair:
  past the shoulders, free when unmasked and tied back or tucked into a hood when masked. A
  feminine build and stance, not the men's.
- **Keep:** the round glasses with flip-up IR lenses, the magenta bandana pulled over her face,
  ink on her fingers.
- **Outfit, Claude's suggestion:** a long secondhand coat, knee-length or longer, layered over
  thrifted pieces, with a scarf, slim trousers and boots. From above, a long coat gives her a
  silhouette nobody else has. Bryson picked "thrifted and layered" for her on 2026-10-12.
- **Colour:** magenta, on the bandana and a few accents.

### The developer (he, mid-40s)

Heavyset, round friendly face, short greying beard, glasses. A former senior engineer, let go with
the last of them; the only one who remembers working before AI. Warm, unflashy, the dad of the
group, and an old-school hacker.

- **Keep:** his face, glasses and the green neck gaiter.
- **Outfit, Claude's suggestion:** a padded vest over a long-sleeved shirt or flannel, worn work
  pants, trail shoes. A vest over sleeves gives a broad, layered silhouette unlike the others; it's
  the tech-dad uniform gone shabby. One nod to the past: an old faded conference lanyard, or a
  cable coiled on a pack strap.
- **Colour:** muted terminal green, on the gaiter and a few accents.

## The packs: computers you wear

Each pack is a real computer, the AI's whole body: a compute unit, batteries, cooling, and a 360°
camera on a short stalk rising above the shoulder, so the AI sees around the Operator. Shaped and
strapped like a backpack, hard shelled, not fabric. No screen, no face, no glow: a small status
LED at most.

Which kind goes with whom is **Claude's suggestion; Bryson to confirm**:

| Operator | Kind | Why | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tradesman | Newer, sleeker | The one with money, and an electrician: he bought good hardware and installed it properly | A compact, clean, moulded shell, rugged industrial grade. Tidy cable runs, a proper vent grille, a short sturdy camera mast. Orange in a detail or two |
| Writer | Cheap and old | Broke; she got what she could afford, secondhand | An old consumer unit past its prime: scuffed plastic, cracked and taped corners, mismatched replacement panels, a thinner wobbly camera stalk held with tape and zip ties, folded paper tucked under a strap |
| Developer | DIY | An engineer who builds his own | An open-frame rig built from salvaged server parts: exposed heat-sink fins, a fan, hand-routed cables, latches and old stickers, a camera on a jury-rigged articulated arm. The heaviest of the three |

The camera should read in every view, including from the battle camera above: a small dome or
ring of lenses on a stalk, sticking up behind the head.
