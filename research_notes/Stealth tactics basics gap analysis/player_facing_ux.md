# Player-facing basics of tactics and stealth-tactics games: a gap analysis against RULES.md (V1)

Scope note: our terms are from RULES.md (V1, 2026-10-04). What RULES.md already guarantees on the player-facing side: cones always visible outside the fog, in two tiers (rule 23); tiles that would get you noticed or seen marked while planning a move (rule 24); a sound-area preview with the visible responding guards marked before a verb is used (rule 18); aim lines (rule 31); the turn order at the top of the screen (rule 1); a caution countdown (rule 29); last-known positions; chip inspection (rule 11); hub-to-circuit lines in the network view; Predict ghost paths. Explicit gaps: "There is no undo in V1" (rule 4), and a loss restarts the current phase with its entry conditions (rule 50). Nothing in RULES.md or DESIGN.md covers saving, onboarding, enemy-turn speed, a history log, a glossary in the UI, difficulty or accessibility.
Source quality: many findings below come from fan wikis, guides, reviews and Steam threads rather than developer documentation, and are flagged where it matters. In "Inferences", Must = should be in the V1 prototype; Nice = later.

## 1. Move and action previews: threat ranges, cones, path costs, "you'll be seen here", enemy intent, outcome previews, danger zones, turn order

### Takeaway
The genre standard is that every consequence the player can't easily work out in their head is shown before they commit: the path cost by colour band, the cover or exposure at the destination, every enemy threat area on a toggle, and the enemy's intent. Into the Breach is the reference for "the enemy's next move is shown, so the turn is a puzzle". Our rules already have the stealth half (cones, noticed/seen tile marking, sound previews, aim lines). What they lack is the AP-cost path preview, an "end of move" exposure summary, an all-guards danger overlay, and outcome previews for shots, stuns and moving devices.

### Cited Findings
- XCOM 2: after a soldier is selected, blue tiles cost one action and yellow tiles are a dash that uses both. The shield icon at the end of the path shows full or half cover, and a red eye warns that an enemy can see the destination and the squad may be revealed — [Pixel Twelve guide](https://pixeltwelve.com/guides/xcom-2-beginner-guide-first-mission-cover-overwatch); [XCOM 2 Fextralife wiki](https://xcom2.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat)
- Sources disagree on the colour of XCOM's flanked indicator: one wiki says a red shield marks flanked cover; Ufopaedia (for XCOM: Enemy Unknown) says the cover icon turns yellow when flanked, and that enemies only show as flanked while your flanking unit is selected — [Fextralife](https://xcom2.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat); contradicted by [Ufopaedia, Cover (EU2012)](https://ufopaedia.org/Cover_(EU2012))
- The XCOM 2 mod "Gotcha" (formerly Flank Preview) extends the line-of-sight indicator so the player can see beforehand whether a move will flank an enemy. Players modded in a preview the base game lacked — [Steam Workshop: Gotcha](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/629910798)
- Fire Emblem: Three Houses shows a highlighted enemy's movement and attack range, and ZR shows the danger radius of every enemy. Awakening has two separate toggles, one for every enemy's reach and one for individual enemies, and they work together. In Engage, ZL shows the whole map's enemy reach, and enemy attack lines appear while you move a unit — [SuperCheats, Three Houses](https://www.supercheats.com/fire-emblem-three-houses/walkthrough/danger-radius); [Siliconera, Awakening](https://www.siliconera.com/2013/01/24/the-things-you-can-do-on-fire-emblem-awakenings-world-map-2/); [Prima Games, Engage](https://primagames.com/tips/how-to-see-enemy-targeting-in-fire-emblem-engage)
- An FE8 fan patch (FogDR) adds a per-enemy danger radius you can pin with Select, separate from the vanilla all-enemies Danger Zone. Players wanted both granularities — [FE8U_FogDR on GitHub](https://github.com/Huichelaar/FE8U_FogDR)
- Into the Breach: a held key (ALT) shows the enemies' attack order and targets; holding CTRL over a unit shows its description, attributes and movement range; almost every element has a mouse-over tooltip — [Gamepressure beginner's guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/into-the-breach/beginners-guide-tips/zfaa76)
- Justin Ma (Subset Games) on Into the Breach: "we would sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time... If it can be shown in a clear way, yes, we can have that" (2018 RPS interview, seen only through a repost) — [repost of the RPS interview](https://reads.mhlakhani.com/posts/582127219_10156233399322220); also [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/entertainment/video-games/breach-mods-soundtrack-mobile-nintendo-860670)
- Zach Gage's GDC 2018 talk on games understood at a glance used Into the Breach as an example of a poster-design principle: the most important information (the grid, the mechs and bugs, fire) registers first, and detail comes in smaller steps — [Gigazine write-up](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20180501-game-design-subway-legibility)
- Triangle Strategy's "Simulation Mode" lets the player check the estimated results of actions against enemies before committing — [RPGFan review](https://www.rpgfan.com/review/triangle-strategy)
- Shadow Tactics: the player clicks one enemy to show its cone, and can place a ground marker that shows which enemies can see that point at any moment. The outer part of a cone lets crouching characters pass unnoticed — [Neowin review](https://www.neowin.net/news/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun-offers-exceptional-gameplay-but-a-forgettable-story); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/418240/discussions/0/1290691937705159737)
- Shadow Tactics players asked for a placeable cone "watcher" that tells apart the parts of a cone that do and don't see a crouching character. Tier legibility was a real complaint. They also said the angled camera makes cone and cover checks harder than a top-down view — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/418240/discussions/0/1290691937705159737)
- Invisible Inc.: a guard's peripheral vision is shaded lighter red and labelled "noticed"; the core is "watched". An alerted guard's cone widens to roughly 180°. A 2017 patch made tiles behind cover show as watched or noticed for specific guard types (Sharpshooters, Surveillance Security), so the tile display tracks each enemy's real perception — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/243970/discussions/0/617336568086845807); [mod/patch changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/569390373?p=7)
- Invisible Inc. bug reports from early access: a tile that should have shown "noticed" was blank and the guard didn't react; a tile sometimes showed "watched" when it should have been "noticed". A mismatch between the display and the logic is the failure mode players report — [Klei forums 40407](https://kleiforums.com/forums/topic/40407-gameplay-camera-sightturret-sightwatched-but-cant-view-from-where/); [Klei forums 58252](https://kleiforums.com/forums/topic/58252-graphics-all-locations-watched-by-the-enemy/)
- Mario + Rabbids Kingdom Battle: a reviewer said enemy movement ranges are on a secondary screen rather than always visible, and that overwatch threats and line of sight were hard to read — [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/?p=196353)

### Inferences
- Must: an AP-cost path preview for Operators, in bands like XCOM's. One band for the tiles reachable on the remaining AP walking; a second for sprint-only tiles, labelled with "costs your shot" because sprinting gives it up (rule 4); the AP left shown at the cursor. Our sprint has an unusual cost (it gives up the shot, and a cut-short sprint is refunded down to walking cost), so the preview has to state it, or players will lose their shot by accident.
- Must: an end-of-move summary on the destination tile, like XCOM's shield and red eye. Icons for: "seen by guard X" or "noticed by X", in the tier that applies after caution and darkness (rules 24, 25, 29); "beyond tether: AI will be pulled out" (Tether); "in aim line of X" (rule 31); "move will stop here: reveals guard" if it can be predicted. Rule 24 marks tiles along the way; the destination needs one consolidated warning, because rule 27 stops a move at the reveal tile and the player needs to know whether the Operator ends in view.
- Must: a danger-zone toggle, like Fire Emblem's, for the union of every visible guard's and camera's seen tier and noticed tier, plus pinnable single-guard overlays. Rule 23 shows the cones, but with 4 or more guards plus cameras the overlapping cones become unreadable (the Mario + Rabbids and Shadow Tactics complaints). Also a toggle for every visible guard's next-turn movement reach (4 tiles), the reach a guard has whether or not it's investigating.
- Must: enemy intent, as in Into the Breach, beyond Predict. Our rules make Predict a chip (2 guards, costs context), so intent is deliberately partial. The free baseline should show the state that drives each guard's next turn: an icon on the guard for patrolling, investigating (with a line to the marked spot), alerted, searching (with the last-known position), stunned (with turns left), and an aim line. This keeps Predict valuable, since it shows paths, while the player always knows what mood a guard is in. Recommend Bryson decide how much patrol-route display is free: Shadow Tactics and Invisible Inc. let you observe it, and Invisible Inc. reveals routes through specific tools.
- Must: outcome previews for deterministic effects that aren't drawn yet. The electric car's activate path, to the tile where it hits something, with the guards it would stun highlighted; the flashbang radius with the guards it would blind; the shot's target with range 5 and line of sight, and "this guard's stun will be found by guard Y next turn" where that's visible. A power-hub preview showing every device on the circuit and every lure it would create. Our circuit lines exist, but the preview should list the lures, because a hub that kills four cameras makes four lures (Devices).
- Must: a hack outcome preview in the network view: the current progress and goal, the progress after this hack with the multipliers applied, "Breaches now → refund" (rule 6) and the context cost. Refund chains are the core skill expression of the network half, and the refund rule (untouched to Breached in one action) is easy to misjudge.
- Nice: a placeable watch marker, like Shadow Tactics', that shows which visible enemies would see or notice a chosen tile now. Our per-tile marking (rule 24) mostly covers this during movement; the marker helps planning for other Operators.
- Nice: Into the Breach's held key for the order and targets of every enemy action, adapted to show the turn-order strip and the visible guards' state icons at once.
- The turn order (rule 1) should show unrevealed enemies as a count or a "?" so the player knows hidden turns are happening. Recommendation only; rule 1 says unrevealed enemies don't appear, so this needs Bryson's call.

### Gaps
- I couldn't retrieve Subset's own account of Into the Breach's telegraphing UI (the GDC 2019 postmortem by Matthew Davis is video only: [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025772/-Into-the-Breach-Design)).
- I found no reliable source on Desperados III's or Shadow Gambit's cone-tier display specifics, nor on Mario + Rabbids' movement-path preview beyond reviews.
- XCOM 2's exact vanilla flanked-icon colour is unresolved (sources conflict).

## 2. Undo and rewind: what the genre does, the consensus for deterministic puzzle-tactics, and what "no undo" costs

### Takeaway
Into the Breach, the touchstone, does not have "no undo". It has free undo of any movement until an action commits, plus one full-turn reset per battle. Fire Emblem (since Echoes, 2017) ships a limited turn-rewind in every game; Invisible Inc. scales its rewind count with difficulty and removes it in Ironman; Old World lets players roll back any action. For a deterministic puzzle where the enemy's response is meant to be readable, a free move-undo before commitment is effectively the consensus. "No undo" turns misclicks and misread previews into a forced phase restart, which in our game could mean replaying many turns.

### Cited Findings
- Into the Breach: you can undo any unit's movement during your turn, but once that unit acts it is locked in. Once per battle, time travel resets every action taken that turn — [Nintendo Life review](https://nintendolife.com/reviews/switch-eshop/into_the_breach)
- A Steam user's description: in effect you can preview the whole turn before committing, moving every unit and previewing every action, and undo it all until you commit to an action — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/590380/discussions/0/2650805212053351822/?ctp=2)
- Player debate on Into the Breach's limit: one player asked for unlimited turn resets; another argued that limited resets create depth because they force you to find the right move order — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/590380/discussions/0/2650805212053351822/?ctp=2)
- A commenter on an itch.io game noted the inconsistency of free movement undo but no undo of abilities. Players notice where the undo line falls — [itch.io comment](https://hempuli.itch.io/mobile-suit-baba/comments?after=80)
- Soren Johnson's Designer Notes episodes with Justin Ma (#70) and Matthew Davis (#71) both debate whether Into the Breach should have unlimited undo. Johnson's own game, Old World, has it; the designers' reasoning wasn't retrievable as text — [Designer Notes #70](https://www.designer-notes.com/designer-notes-70-justin-ma/); [Designer Notes #71](https://www.designer-notes.com/designer-notes-71-matthew-davis/)
- In Old World, "Literally every action... can be immediately rolled back with no consequences" — cited in [PC Gamer, "Put an 'undo' button in every strategy game"](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/put-an-undo-button-in-every-strategy-game) (article body not retrievable; claim as surfaced in search summary)
- Invisible Inc.: rewinds come by difficulty. Five on the basic difficulty, adjustable; harder modes give fewer rewinds and turn off mission retries; Ironman removes rewinds — [waytoomany.games review](https://waytoomany.games/2020/06/20/review-invisible-inc-switch/); [Slant](https://slantmagazine.com/games/invisible-inc). According to players, setting rewinds to 99 with the other presets left alone doesn't mark the run as custom (user report, not official) — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/243970/discussions/0/154645539344256226)
- Fire Emblem Echoes' Mila's Turnwheel rewinds whole turns for both armies. It starts at 3 charges and rises to 12 with Cogs; the history is long but finite, and the game warns before it runs out. It was framed as a middle ground between permadeath and none, and a turn-rewind has been in every Fire Emblem since. One pre-release guide claims 3 uses per map, refilled when the map ends, which conflicts with the wiki — [Fire Emblem Wiki](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Mila%27s_Turnwheel_II); [The Sixth Axis review](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=287954); contradicted by [Samurai Gamers guide](https://samurai-gamers.com/fire-emblem-echoes-shadows-valentia-wiki/milas-turnwheel-guide/)
- Triangle Strategy: no undo was found. At launch there was no in-game option to restart a battle (players soft-reset), and the autosave has one slot. Its demo-feedback patch notes added a battle retry and a quicksave made automatically every set number of turns — [Attack of the Fanboy](https://attackofthefanboy.com/guides/how-to-restart-battles-in-triangle-strategy/); [Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/triangle-strategy/permanent-events-28); [Nova Crystallis](https://novacrystallis.com/2021/09/triangle-strategy-shares-changes-built-on-demo-versions-survey/)
- A Mario + Rabbids review criticised the lack of an action undo: it was easy to move a character somewhere you didn't mean to — [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/?p=196353)

### Inferences
- Must, as a recommendation for Bryson to decide, since it changes an agreed rule: replace "There is no undo in V1" with an Into the Breach split. (a) Free undo of Operator movement, and of robot movement, while nothing new has happened: no reveal (rule 27), no alert, no sound and no shot since the move. (b) Anything that reveals information or makes the world respond commits: a reveal, a peek, a breach, a verb use, a shot, entering a cone. That keeps hidden information honest; undoing a move that revealed a guard would be a free scout. The rule is "you can undo anything that taught you nothing".
- Network half: undo of AI movement is cheap (Jump hops reveal nothing once the network is known). Hacks, verbs and chip loads commit, because they change context and the world.
- Nice: a once-per-battle turn reset, like Into the Breach's, or a small number by difficulty, like Invisible Inc. A diegetic frame suits our setting: the AI "simulates" or forks the turn, which ties to the AI and context themes. Shadow Gambit shows that a fictional frame removes the "cheating" stigma (see section 3).
- What "no undo" costs here specifically. Rule 50 restarts the whole phase. Our phases are long: the facility mission is 2 phases, each many rounds with 3 Operators, 2 robots and an AI each. A misclick on a sprint (which loses the shot) or a mis-hovered tile then costs a long replay, the misclick frustration the Mario + Rabbids review describes. Determinism raises the stakes: when the outcome was knowable, failure from a misread preview feels like a UI failure rather than a tactical one.
- Implementation note for Bryson and agents: undo is cheap only if the battle state can be snapshotted. Taking a full state snapshot at each commit point in V1 also enables the turn reset, the checkpoint saves of section 3 and the replay/history of section 5. Architecture choice, to be designed together per AGENTS.md.

### Gaps
- The exact reasoning Subset gives for limiting the reset to once per battle isn't in text form (podcast only).
- No source found for a measured cost of "no undo" (e.g., retention or refund data); evidence is reviews and forum complaints.

## 3. Save systems, mid-mission saving, checkpoints, restart granularity

### Takeaway
Two camps. The real-time stealth-tactics games (Shadow Tactics, Desperados III, Shadow Gambit) make quicksave and quickload a core verb, nag you to use it, and make loading fast. The turn-based games (Invisible Inc., XCOM Ironman, Into the Breach) limit or remove saves and lean on rewinds or resets. Whichever camp a game is in, players expect two things: a mid-mission save so they can quit and resume, and a restart finer than "the whole map". Our rules have only a full-phase restart, and no save is specified.

### Cited Findings
- Mimimi (Moritz Wagner, Head of Design) designed save-scumming as an "integral part of the game", wanting players to feel free to experiment, which goes back to Shadow Tactics. Players still felt it was cheap. A rewind with no in-world reason was "fourth-wall breaking", so Shadow Gambit gave it a fiction: the ghost ship returns Afia to a saved "memory". Quicksave and quickload are ability-styled buttons, with a bell chime and a green screen effect; load time is hidden behind a pearl-cracking animation. "failure doesn't matter in our games, because you can just try again", which let them build more intricate encounters — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-shadow-gambit-the-cursed-crew-was-able-to-make-save-scumming-a-central-mechanic)
- Shadow Gambit's save system had to save reliably at any moment, save quickly, and load even faster; its architecture dates from Shadow Tactics' four-person team — [Game Developer deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/programming/deep-dive-creating-and-fine-tuning-the-save-system-of-_shadow-gambit_)
- Desperados III: a reminder to save pops up after a period without saving, adjustable to 1, 3, 5 or 10 minutes or off (Options > Gameplay). There's no autosave, because quicksaving is part of the tactical play; the custom difficulty can also limit the number of saves — [God is a Geek tips](https://godisageek.com/2020/06/10-desperados-iii-tips-to-help-you-out-in-the-wild-west/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/610370/discussions/0/2451595501488396622); [Game Chronicles review](https://gamechronicles.com/desperados-iii-review-pc)
- Shadow Tactics' quicksave slots and manual saves make backtracking practical, but the game doesn't lower difficulty after a death — [GameGeeker review](https://gamegeeker.com/pt/games/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun/review); [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/reviews/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun-review)
- Invisible Inc.: the harder modes turn off mission retries (continues) on top of reducing rewinds — [Slant review](https://slantmagazine.com/games/invisible-inc)
- Triangle Strategy's demo feedback led to a quicksave made automatically every set number of turns — [Nova Crystallis](https://novacrystallis.com/2021/09/triangle-strategy-shares-changes-built-on-demo-versions-survey/)

### Inferences
- Must, for V1 testing alone: a "suspend" save that lets the player quit mid-phase and resume exactly there. It isn't a scumming tool: the file loads once, or is overwritten each turn like Into the Breach's single save. Without it, every playtest session has to end at a phase boundary.
- Must: restarts finer than the phase. Proposal: "Restart phase" (rule 50) plus "Restart from start of round N" for the last few rounds, built on the turn snapshots from section 2. Into the Breach effectively saves at the start of every turn; Triangle Strategy added turn-based autosaves after its demo feedback.
- Nice: a decision for Bryson about which camp we're in. Our game is turn-based and deterministic, which favours Into the Breach's commit-and-reset over Mimimi's quicksave culture. Free quicksave plus determinism makes a brute-force solver of the player (save, try a lure, load). The Mimimi lesson still applies: if any rewind exists, give it a fiction (the AI forks a simulation) and make it fast.
- A defeat now triggers only when all 3 Operators are downed (rule 50). A one-Operator-down state is legal but may be unwinnable or miserable; consider offering "Restart phase" in the pause menu at all times, plus a "retry from last round" prompt when an Operator goes down. That's a UI offer, not a rule change.

### Gaps
- No source found on XCOM 2 Ironman's exact save behaviour this session (known in general that Ironman uses a single continuous autosave; unverified here).
- No data on how often players use quicksave in Mimimi games (they've said in talks that they track it; not retrieved).

## 4. Onboarding: teaching interlocking systems

### Takeaway
I found little hard documentation of how Into the Breach's or Invisible Inc.'s tutorials are structured. What is documented: Invisible Inc. sets expectations ("you will die") and teaches through repeated failure, escalating its alarm in visible steps; Into the Breach keeps every rule visible on the board and in hover text, so the tutorial can stay short; Shadow Tactics reveals optional challenges only after the first clear. Our game has two simultaneous layers (map and network) and many interlocking rules, so the risk is greater than in either touchstone, and introducing one mechanic per map is the standard fix.

### Cited Findings
- Invisible Inc.'s tutorial warns players they will die many times; reviewers call the game unfriendly to beginners, and say each failure is a lesson that builds skill — [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/invisible-inc-review); [Gamepressure review](https://www.gamepressure.com/editorials/reviews/review-of-invisible-inc-a-demanding-and-inventive-mix-of-strategy/z835)
- Invisible Inc.'s alarm tracker rises every turn, and each level adds a stated complication (level 1: more cameras online; level 2: turrets; level 3: new guards). It is a visible, legible ratchet that teaches urgency without a tutorial screen — [Geeks Under Grace review](https://www.geeksundergrace.com/gaming/pc/review-invisible-inc/); [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/games/invisible_inc/b/macintosh/archive/2015/05/13/invisible-inc-game-informer-review)
- Into the Breach's in-game tutorials and guides change the unit icons they show to match the active squad (from the repost of the RPS interview) — [repost](https://reads.mhlakhani.com/posts/582127219_10156233399322220)
- Shadow Tactics' badges (optional goals, such as not swimming or not killing) appear only after a mission is completed on normal difficulty, so first plays aren't overloaded — [GamePro.de review](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun-die-rueckkehr-der-echtzeit-taktik,3317431,seite2.html)
- Mimimi said its studio aimed to make stealth more accessible while keeping the challenge for fans — [Set Side B interview](https://setsideb.com/mimimi-games-developer-interview/)

### Inferences
- Must: an introduction order, one interlocking system per map. A suggested ladder for the tutorial battle and the first missions: (1) moving and AP, cones and the noticed/seen tiers with no network; (2) shot, stun, tie up, carry, receptacle; (3) deploy the AI, one access point, tether, breach a door; (4) refunds and a camera breach (a camera stops reporting the team, a big moment); (5) sound lures and the sound preview; (6) light and dark, then a blackout; (7) chips, context and compaction; (8) robots as relays, shared compute; (9) caution and aim. The facility mission currently teaches all of it at once. STORY.md has the protagonist's new AI starting general, with the questionnaire after the tutorial, which fits a gradual reveal of the AI half.
- Must: text that names what happened when something new triggers, the first time only. "Noticed: the guard will investigate this tile on its turn", "Refund: a one-action breach returns the AP". It shows only on the first occurrence.
- Nice: an Invisible Inc.-style statement of the stakes. Our caution countdown (rule 29) is the nearest equivalent; label it with its rule effect ("Caution: noticed counts as seen, 3 rounds") rather than just a number.
- Nice: badges or challenges revealed after the first clear, like Shadow Tactics: no alerts, no stuns, no compaction, all three Operators extracted. They give the optional caches (rule 51, V2) a sibling for replay value.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on the structure of Into the Breach's tutorial, Shadow Tactics' first-mission pacing, or a GDC talk on teaching tactics systems. Recommend watching the GDC 2019 Into the Breach postmortem ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025772/-Into-the-Breach-Design)) if this matters.

## 5. Feedback and game feel: enemy-turn speed, camera, sound cues, history log, tooltips, glossary, briefing and debrief

### Takeaway
Players expect a way to speed up or skip enemy turns, hover detail on everything, and readable alert feedback. The most-requested quality-of-life mods in XCOM 2 are about turn speed and waiting. Our rules generate many unseen enemy turns (hidden enemies act out of sight, rule 1) and many guards. Without speed controls and a history log the player will be waiting, and won't know what changed.

### Cited Findings
- XCOM 2 has a "Zip Mode" in its gameplay options. The fan mod "Stop Wasting My Time" cut most pauses (one from about 3 seconds to 0.6) and removed its own unit-speed boost in favour of Zip Mode. Players disagree on whether Zip Mode cuts too little or too much — [Steam Workshop changelog](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/changelog/620600092); [Reddit via redlib](https://redlib.hackliberty.org/r/XCOM2/comments/1lf0u5u/how_do_i_get_the_game_to_run_faster)
- Shadow Gambit gives saving a sound (a bell chime) and a visual effect, so a system event is felt — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-shadow-gambit-the-cursed-crew-was-able-to-make-save-scumming-a-central-mechanic)
- Into the Breach: almost every element shows a tooltip on mouse-over, and held keys show more detail — [Gamepressure guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/into-the-breach/beginners-guide-tips/zfaa76)
- Slay the Spire: a reviewer praised the at-a-glance legibility, with every shop item explained on mouse-over. A 2018 Steam thread still asked for colour bands and type sorting to make cards easier to tell apart — [Shrunken Shrine retrospective](https://shrunkenshrine.com/2023/09/10/rdbg-11-slay-the-spire-2017/); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1692659135907302948)
- Generic tooltip practice: a tooltip should also appear on keyboard or controller focus, not only on mouse hover — [UX Planet](https://uxplanet.org/tooltip-guidelines-best-practices-and-common-mistakes-with-examples-bde5993048f0)
- Shadow Tactics' badges (optional goals per mission) are a post-mission rating layer — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_Tactics:_Blades_of_the_Shogun); [GamePro.de](https://www.gamepro.de/artikel/shadow-tactics-blades-of-the-shogun-die-rueckkehr-der-echtzeit-taktik,3317431,seite2.html)

### Inferences
- Must: enemy-turn speed. Settings for normal, fast and instant, and a key that skips the current enemy's animation. The camera should frame each visible enemy's action only when something the player cares about changes (a new investigation, an aim line, a body found, a camera spotting); routine patrol steps run in parallel, or quickly, without the camera moving. Hidden enemies' turns (rule 1) should take no screen time beyond a short "unseen units acting" tick.
- Must: a round-by-round event log. "Guard 3 noticed Operator B at (x,y) → investigating", "Camera 2 spotted C → closest guard sent", "Guard 1 aims at Protagonist", "Refund: +1 AP", "Context 74 → 89". In a deterministic puzzle, learning from a failure needs the causal chain. It can also carry the "why did that happen" for rules like 21 (a camera sighting sends a guard, never an alert) and 36 (a found body raises a full alert).
- Must: a distinct audio and visual cue for each perception change: noticed (soft), seen or alerted (sharp), an aim line drawn, a body found, caution starting and ending. Since the noticed/seen distinction is the heart of the stealth model, it should be audible as well as coloured (this is also the accessibility double-coding of section 6).
- Must: hover detail everywhere, as in Into the Breach. On a guard: state, turns stunned, investigation target, aim. On a device: verbs, sound radius and who responds (closest or all), circuit, goal and progress. On a tile: light or dark, zone, and which cones reach it. RULES.md already holds every term twice (glossary plus full definition); surface the glossary entry in the UI as linked keywords, so hovering "Refund" in a tooltip shows the glossary line. This makes the docs part of the deliverable.
- Nice: a mission briefing screen (objective, phases, extraction, known devices) and a debrief: rounds taken, alerts, stuns, tie-ups, Breaches, refunds, peak context, Operators downed, and badges. Hitman-style challenges are a familiar model (not sourced this session).
- Nice: an "end turn with AP left" confirmation when the Operator or AI still has AP, a common tactics quality-of-life prompt. With two pools per unit (rule 5), forgetting the AI's 2 AP will be common.

- No source found on enemy-turn skip options in Invisible Inc. or Into the Breach.

## 6. Difficulty modes and accessibility

### Takeaway
The stealth-tactics games treat difficulty as a set of separable dials (Invisible Inc.'s rewinds, retries, alarm and modifiers; Desperados III's custom detection speed, saves and Showdown pause), and the accessibility guidelines all say the same first thing: no essential information carried by colour alone. Our whole stealth model is red/yellow-style cone tiers, coloured tiles and lines. That makes double-coding a must-have from the first prototype, not polish.

### Cited Findings
- Invisible Inc. has "a ton of difficulty settings and modifiers": rewinds, mission retries, campaign time, an Ironman mode that removes rewinds, and an Endless mode — [waytoomany.games](https://waytoomany.games/2020/06/20/review-invisible-inc-switch/); [Slant](https://slantmagazine.com/games/invisible-inc)
- Desperados III's custom difficulty adjusts enemy setups, player health, ammo, detection speed and the number of saves, and whether Showdown Mode pauses. A reviewer called its accessibility and customisation "one of the best I've seen in a long time" — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/610370/discussions/0/2451595501488396622); [Twinfinite review](https://twinfinite.net/reviews/desperados-iii-review/)
- Game Accessibility Guidelines: ensure no essential information is conveyed by a fixed colour alone; pair colour with text, symbol, pattern or shape, ideally on by default; palette swaps alone are limited; allow colour-dependent elements to be customised — [GAG](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/ensure-no-essential-information-is-conveyed-by-a-fixed-colour-alone/)
- Xbox Accessibility Guideline 103: aim for no cue to depend on colour alone; where it can't be avoided, let players pick colours per element — [Xbox XAG 103](https://devdocs.xbox.com/gaming/accessibility/xbox-accessibility-guidelines/103)
- Can I Play That: audit every place where colour carries information (UI, puzzle elements, lights) and back it up with sound, shape and animation; colourblind simulators don't replace testing with players — [Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/?p=8897); [Colorblind Games](https://colorblindgames.com/2021/08/02/colorblind-gaming-101-some-solutions)
- Fire Emblem's casual mode (fallen allies return after each battle) is the series' alternative to permadeath alongside the Turnwheel — [The Sixth Axis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/?p=287954)

### Inferences
- Must: double-code the cone tiers and tile warnings. Seen = a solid fill plus an eye glyph; noticed = a hatched or dotted fill plus a "?" glyph; aim line = a dashed line with a crosshair at the target. Caution (noticed counts as seen) should change the pattern, not just the hue. Dark tiles must stay readable against "fogged" and "greyed" tiles (rule 25 greys out the Operator's view beyond their dark window like fog, so dark and fog currently look the same by rule). Separate glyphs are needed for "dark, but you see it" versus "fogged".
- Must: the enemy-red against white/black/red art direction (DESIGN.md: OpenBrain is white with red accents) collides with red as the warning colour. Flag for Bryson: choose the cone and warning palette so it doesn't fight the faction colours, and use pattern and glyph as the primary carriers.
- Nice: difficulty as separate dials rather than presets only, following Invisible Inc. and Desperados: the number of turn resets (0, 1, 3, unlimited), whether a move-undo exists, the caution length (rule 29, currently 3), guard search length, stun length, and an "Ironman" toggle. Our numbers are already tagged placeholder, which makes them natural dials. Recommend leaving the V1 rules fixed and exposing these only as debug or tuning options for playtests.
- Nice: text size, remappable keys (we already have many hotkeys: H, L, I, N, Space), a hold-versus-toggle choice for overlays, and a reduced-motion option for camera moves during enemy turns.
- DESIGN.md says "No permadeath" and Operators downed in a battle are only out for that battle. That already sits at Fire Emblem's casual end, so the difficulty conversation is about undo, resets and information, not losses.

### Gaps
- No source found on XCOM 2's or Shadow Tactics' colourblind options specifically, nor on any stealth-tactics game's colourblind cone palette.
- No source retrieved on screen-reader or motor accessibility in turn-based tactics (e.g., Can I Play That reviews of Into the Breach); worth a targeted follow-up.
